# Companies that will be in everything (9 Nov 2026)

A second list for the study. The question: are there companies so central to the digital world and to infrastructure that almost everything new has to pass through them? If yes, are they worth buying on their own, or is VWCE enough?

All of them are already inside VWCE. This is a paper study only. No money goes in before the review in January 2028.

## My five for the study (10 Nov 2026)
P/E alone is not enough, so I scored each candidate from 1 to 5 on five things: how much the world depends on it, growth, profits and cash returned to shareholders (dividends and buybacks), price, and risk (5 means low risk). My own judgment from research, out of 25.

| Company | Depends on it | Growth | Profits and returns | Price | Risk | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 21 |
| Nvidia | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 20 |
| TSMC | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 20 |
| Visa | 4 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 19 |
| Broadcom | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 19 |
| Alphabet | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 19 |
| Eli Lilly | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 18 |
| KLA | 4 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 18 |
| ASML | 5 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 17 |

Final five: Microsoft, Nvidia, TSMC, Visa and Eli Lilly. Not just the top five by score: I want them spread out. Two chip companies, one software and cloud, one payments, one health. Broadcom would make three chip companies, and I'm not comfortable with Alphabet's search risk.

- Microsoft: cloud and AI for companies, Office and Windows everywhere. The steadiest
- Nvidia: over 90% of AI accelerators, CUDA has millions of developers. Risks: Google, Amazon and Microsoft making their own chips, China, the Nov 2026 investigation of its CEO
- TSMC: makes the chips for almost everyone, Nvidia's custom rivals included, so it wins either way. Taiwan risk
- Visa: a small cut of each card payment, very little capital needed
- Eli Lilly: leader in obesity and diabetes drugs with Novo Nordisk, the fastest growing big drug company. Expensive (forward P/E about 37) and drug prices are a political target

Bench: Broadcom, KLA, Alphabet, ASML.

Also checked and kept in the study only: Amazon (AWS, about a third of the cloud market), Apple (over 2 billion devices, slow growth, expensive), GE Vernova and Siemens Energy (gas turbines sold out for years, very expensive after the run), Novo Nordisk (Lilly's rival in obesity drugs, much cheaper after a big fall, but losing ground), SAP (the software that runs big companies) and Mastercard (same as Visa, a bit more expensive).

## Left out on purpose
- Astera Labs: young, expensive, few clients
- Arm: great business, price needs years of perfection
- Lasertec: one product, very volatile
- Ajinomoto: great niche, but you mostly buy a food company
- Oklo: no revenue yet
- Equinix: high debt, and my DCF does not suit this kind of company

If the screener shows one of the five as AVOID or far too expensive, I swap it and write down why.

## What I expect
- Most of these are great businesses. That is known, so most are priced as great businesses
- The study tests if buying them on their own beats VWCE after the price I pay
- Research says about 4% of stocks create all the long term gains (Bessembinder). These look like candidates, but many past "sure things" fell behind (Cisco, Intel, GE)

## How I judge them in January 2028
- Total return of each company and of the whole list against VWCE, same dates
- How many beat VWCE, and by how much
- If the list did not clearly beat VWCE after two years, I keep ETFs only
