# Simpler plan: VWCE and 8 stocks (12 Nov 2026)

## What changed
- From 60% VWCE, 10% small caps, 8% theme ETFs and 22% stocks to 73% VWCE and 27% stocks
- Out before the first purchase: the small cap ETF, the three theme ETFs, Safran and Microsoft
- In: ASML and GE Vernova at 3% each
- Nvidia and Mastercard go to 5%, Eli Lilly to 4%
- Intuitive Surgical keeps its shares but gets no new money
- One fair price method for every company, with the yearly return next to it (future/fair_price.py)
- No sector above 20% of the portfolio, counting only my stocks
- BNY was in the plan but Trade Republic has no savings plan for it, so its 3% went to VWCE. It stays on the watchlist for a one-off buy below about 143 dollars

## Why
- I was changing the portfolio almost every day. The problem was not the method, it was me. Now plans only change in the quarterly review or after a full written analysis
- Small caps: the extra return is weak without a quality filter (Asness and others, 2018) and they are the opposite of the companies I look for
- Theme ETFs: specialised ETFs lost about 30% against the market in their first 5 years, because the theme was already expensive at launch (Ben-David and others, 2023). The grid ETF costs 0.63% a year and had gone up a lot
- Safran, Microsoft and Alphabet are good businesses above my fair price today. They wait on the watchlist with an entry price
- ASML: record sales and margin in Q3 2026, 2026 sales guided to 44 to 45 billion euros (32.7 in 2025), 30% more EUV capacity planned for 2027. About 8% below my fair price
- GE Vernova: orders up 96% in Q3 2026 and a backlog of 197 billion dollars. It is my direct bet on energy now that the grid ETF is out

## What I accept
- GE Vernova is the riskiest stock I own: a cyclical business, tied to data centers, with cash flow that today comes mostly from customer down payments. In my careful case it is worth less than its price
- Broadcom is the one I am least sure about. My old screener calls it expensive and my new fair price only works if it gets close to its AI targets. I use a careful growth of 12% for it
- Mastercard and Eli Lilly are at their fair price, not below. They give me about 9% a year in my base case, no more
- If I am right about everything, the stocks add maybe half a point a year to the whole portfolio. If I am wrong, the January 2030 test sends the money back to VWCE
- Nvidia: the board is looking into its CEO and may replace him. I keep the plan and only buy extra if the price falls 15% below my fair price with the business intact
