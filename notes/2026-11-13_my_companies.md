# 13 Nov 2026: my companies back, the better method kept

The review of 13 Nov fixed real mistakes in the fair price method, and I keep every one of those fixes (notes/2026-11-13_method_and_portfolio.md). But it also swapped my companies for new ones in a single evening: ResMed, Schneider Electric and S&P Global in, Vertiv and Vistra out. That is exactly the kind of move I do not want to make. I invest in companies I know and trust, and a new name needs a written analysis first.

## What I kept from the review
- The DCF no longer grows the next 12 months twice
- Growth is my own number, capped at 15%, not picked to hit a price
- The fair price is 60% my DCF and 40% Morningstar, and the rate follows the 10 year yield
- Extra buys need the full margin of safety (15%, 25% or 35%) and two checks
- An alarm switched on stops every buy of that company

## What I changed back
- My portfolio: Nvidia 5, TSMC 5, ASML 5, Broadcom 4, Vertiv 4, GE Vernova 4, Vistra 3, Mastercard 5, Eli Lilly 5, Safran 5, Alphabet 3. VWCE 52% ideal, 62% today
- Mastercard (16 to 19% above fair) and Eli Lilly (about 40% above fair) stay in the mix but their plans are paused. I keep the shares I have. Their money goes to VWCE until the price comes back
- ResMed, Schneider Electric and S&P Global go to the watchlist. Good businesses, but I have not studied them myself
- No more "only one big tech name". Each priority comes in when it reaches its own fair price

## Nvidia
I trust Nvidia more than any other company here. So it can grow up to 10% of the portfolio, not 5%. The extra money only goes in when it is really cheap: 35% below my fair price, because Morningstar rates it very hard to value. Today that is about 146 USD against 224 fair.

The alarm stays on until I read the results of 17 Nov and see a clear plan for the CEO. I switch it off when the data center outlook holds, there are no formal charges and the leadership is stable. I do not need to wait two full quarters.
