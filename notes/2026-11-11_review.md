# Portfolio review (11 Nov 2026)

## What changed
- Safran in at 3%: aircraft engines, in euros, doesn't depend on AI spending. Revenue +14.7% in Q3 2026, outlook for 2026 confirmed. FAIR in the screener, about 25 times next year's profit
- Eli Lilly in at 3%: obesity and diabetes drugs, a duopoly with Novo Nordisk. Revenue +39% in Q3 2026, 2026 outlook raised to 91 to 92 billion dollars. About 40 times this year's profit. The DCF doesn't work for it (free cash flow far below profit), so I look at the forward P/E
- Quantum ETF out: pure quantum companies lose money and trade at many times their sales. I follow them in the screener and look again in January
- Health ETF 4% to 3% (Lilly is already 11% of it), nuclear 3% to 2%, Intuitive Surgical 3% to 2% until its January test
- New rule: the AI chip stocks (TSMC, Broadcom, Nvidia) stay under 12% together
- Bets go from 3 to 5% each to 2 to 5% each, and from 5 to 7 stocks at most
- 32 companies added to the screener: aviation, defense, nuclear, chips, health, data, industry, rail and waste

## Why
My stocks were almost all AI chips and all in dollars. A crash in AI spending would have hit four of them at once. Safran and Lilly add growth that comes from somewhere else.

## Why not more
General Dynamics waits for January: it takes Intuitive's place if Intuitive fails its test. Hoya, ASML, Alphabet, GE Vernova and Autodesk were good but either too expensive or more of the same AI cycle.

## What I did not do
No one-off buy of Safran or Lilly. Neither is cheap, so the monthly plans are enough, with no fee.
