## How I invest

**Every month**
- About one sixth of the month goes to VWCE (Vanguard FTSE All-World) in an automatic plan. It never stops: it is the part for retirement in 50 years.
- The other five sixths are for stocks, but only when there is a clean signal. If there is none, the money waits in a cash reserve.
- Small automatic plans go to companies at a good price (today TSMC and Nvidia), at most 4 at a time. On the 1st of every month I check if each plan stays, pauses or starts.
- Bigger buys are 1 or 2 orders, never many small ones, because a fixed fee per order eats small buys.
- The reserve has a cap. What goes above it goes to VWCE the next month.

**A clean signal means all of this**
- The company passes the five gates of the ranking.
- Analysts raised their earnings estimates over the last 90 days.
- No ⚠ (analysts cutting estimates) and no results in the next 7 days.
- The price is at an alert or close enough to the fair value for a first buy.

**Using the reserve**
- When the VIX is 30 or more, up to a third of the reserve. At 40 or more, all of it.
- A company with 🔥 can get up to a third, once a month.
- Only companies without ⚠ and with estimates going up.

## The weekly ranking

Every Monday 64 large companies in AI, chips, energy, health, cybersecurity and a few others go through five gates: market value, daily trading, number of analysts, profit and operating cash flow. The ones that pass get 18 criteria worth 0 or 1. Analyst revisions weigh double, because estimates moving up are the best sign that the business is doing better than expected. Then come growth, quality (margins, return on equity, free cash flow, debt, dilution), price (PEG), trend and insider buying.

🔥 Opportunity: estimates going up, a cheap PEG, a fall of 15% or more from the 52 week high, my fair value above the price and analysts agreeing with each other.

## How I value a company

Each company I care about has its own 10 year DCF, built the way investment banks do it.

1. **Free cash flow:** operating profit after tax, plus depreciation, minus capex and working capital. Year 1 growth is the analysts' consensus; the rest fades to the long run rate.
2. **Discount rate (WACC):** CAPM with an adjusted beta and the current 10 year Treasury yield.
3. **Terminal value** with the Gordon formula, checked against how much of the value it carries.
4. **Three scenarios:** bear, base and bull, each with its own growth and margins.
5. **Checks:** a WACC × terminal growth table and the WACC that today's price implies (a reverse DCF).

**Alerts:** if the price is above the base value, alert 1 is halfway down to it and alert 2 is the base value. If it is already below, the alerts are 15% and 30% under today's price.

## Limits

- AI chips and hardware up to about 60% of the money in stocks. Until 31 March 2027 the plans can push it higher while the portfolio is small, but big buys go to other themes.
- Never buy with ⚠, with estimates falling or in the week before results. Conviction does not relax the rules.
- Orders always with a limit price: US stocks between 16:00 and 20:00 Lisbon time, European ones and VWCE between 9:00 and 16:00.

## Discipline

- I never change a DCF scenario on a day of a big rise or fall. Models change after quarterly results.
- Bad news about a person does not stop the plan. News that breaks the business (fraud, losing a key client, a big profit cut) stops every buy until the next results.

## Did it work?

A ghost portfolio follows these rules strictly on paper and is compared every week with the same money only in VWCE. My real portfolio gets the same test. If my stocks lose to VWCE for long, the money goes to VWCE.
