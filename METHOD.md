## How I invest

**Every month**
- A fixed amount goes in on the 2nd with automatic plans on Trade Republic.
- A fixed part goes to VWCE (Vanguard FTSE All-World). It is the base of everything and it never stops.
- A small part goes to Bitcoin while it is under 5% of the portfolio. Then it stops and that money goes to VWCE.
- The rest is a pot for stocks. Each company in the plan has its share of the pot, but it only gets it if the price is within its limit.
- What the stocks do not use stays in cash, up to a cap. Above the cap it goes to VWCE, so money never sits idle for long.

**Extra buys**
On any day a company I trust falls far below its fair value, or falls a lot from its 52 week high while below fair value, I can buy more: some money from my pocket plus half of the cash reserve. Never in a company with its alarm on, and never in one that fails the quality test.

## How I value a company

Each company has its own DCF in my Excel model, built the way investment banks do it.

1. **Free cash flow** for 10 years: operating profit after tax, plus depreciation, minus capex. Year 1 revenue growth is the analysts' consensus; years 2 to 5 and the margins are my judgment.
2. **Discount rate (WACC)** with CAPM. The beta is not the stock's own beta, which is noisy, but the sector's: I take the betas of similar companies, remove the effect of their debt, use the median and add back the debt of my company.
3. **Terminal value** after year 10 with the value driver formula: growth only creates value if the new capital earns more than it costs.
4. **Three scenarios** (bear 25%, base 50%, bull 25%). The fair value is the weighted average.
5. **Checks:** a sensitivity table, a reverse DCF (the growth today's price already assumes), an exit multiple and the multiples of similar companies. When the DCF and the multiples disagree by more than 30%, I find out why before I buy.
6. **Revenue by business** for Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, where growth depends on the mix (cloud vs the rest).

## How strict I am with each company

The less certain the value, the bigger the discount I ask for. Uncertainty = (bull value minus bear value) divided by the fair value.

| Strictness | When | The plan buys up to |
|---|---|---|
| Core | Uncertainty up to 55% and a stable business | 10% above fair value (half pace up to 20%) |
| Normal | In between | 10% above fair value |
| Strict | Uncertainty above 75% or a risky business | The fair value |

## Quality test

Five points: revenue growth of at least 10%, operating margin of at least 20%, free cash flow of at least 5% of revenue, net debt of at most 2x EBITDA, and no more than 2% new shares a year. Four points or more to get money.

## My rules

1. I update the data on the 1st of every month and the company models after each quarterly result.
2. I never change a scenario to make a stock look cheap, and never on a day of a big rise or fall.
3. Bad news about people does not stop the plan. News that breaks the business (fraud, losing a key client, a big profit cut, no leadership) stops every buy until the next results.
4. I never sell because the price fell.
5. VWCE stays the base. If a model is wrong about one company, the whole market is still there.
